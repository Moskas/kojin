# Thermal paste change

In order to change the thermal paste, you need to basically disassemble the whole bottom part of the laptop. Thankfully, the screen and its hinges aren't part of the components that need to be disassembled.  
As with older ThinkPads, you need to unscrew the keyboard first. Then you can unplug it from the top and proceed to unscrew every screw from the bottom. The only thing you can omit is the RAM cover, as there are no screws underneath it.  
After that, you need to unplug the palm rest's fingerprint scanner, if you have one, and then lift the top cover. Then it's just a matter of unscrewing a couple of screws from the metal brackets over the SATA drive connector and LCD connector.  
![Top of mainboard with heatsink](/static/img/garden/mainboard_top_x60.jpg)

Don't miss the screws that are partially hidden in the expansion card cage on the bottom left (I didn't see them at first, and it was a bit frustrating since the heatsink is screwed to it as well).  

Finally, you can lift and flip over the mainboard and unscrew the remaining heatsink screws.  
![Bottom of mainboard with heatsink](/static/img/garden/mainboard_bottom_x60.jpg)

> Yes, I did place the mainboard on the LCD while I was unscrewing the heatsink and changing its thermal paste.

If the Wi-Fi kill switch falls out during the process, install it before fitting the mainboard back into the chassis. I've wasted too much time trying to put it back with the mainboard already inside the chassis.

## tl;dr

1. Unscrew the keyboard screws and remove the keyboard.
2. Remove the palm rest and bottom cover screws, then remove the palm rest.
3. Unscrew the SATA connector shield, ExpressCard cage screws, and LCD connector, then lift the shield along with all antenna cables.
4. Lift the PCB and unscrew the remaining heatsink screws.
5. Reassemble everything in reverse order.
